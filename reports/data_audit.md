# Data audit

Generated by `ransomdet prepare`. Runtime 76.8 s, peak memory 2460 MB.

## Checkpoint summary

### Proposed split

I propose candidate C: train on everything before 2016-07-01, validate on July to December 2016, and test on 2017-2018. The starting proposal (A) trains on 7 families and 3,465 ransomware addresses, and 99% of its validation ransomware rows and 100% of its test rows come from families it never saw in training. That turns the main test into an unseen-family test and leaves model selection tuned on families absent from training. The generalization tests already measure unseen families properly.

Under C, training holds 16 families and 11,137 ransomware addresses, validation has 6,786 ransomware rows (4% from families absent in training) and test has 3,424 (5%).

2018 contributes only 3 ransomware rows, so the test period is in practice 2017. I would keep 2018 in test for the false-positive rate and say this in the report.

### Proposed K

I propose K = 10 alerts per day, reported as pooled precision (hits / (10 x days)). Test days average 5.02 ransomware rows and 452 of 695 test days have none, so even a perfect ranker reaches only 0.272 at K = 10. The report will print that ceiling next to the model's number.

### What Ransomwhere can support

Every one of the 21,790 transactions has an amount and a timestamp, so income and neighbors can be rebuilt per address and day. Before that, 2,541 repeated (address, hash) rows need dropping, and 11,334 transactions under 0.3 BTC are removed to match BitcoinHeist's filter.

| feature | rebuildable | how / why not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| income | yes | sum of incoming amounts per address and UTC-6 day, after the 0.3 BTC filter |
| neighbors | yes | number of incoming transactions per address and UTC-6 day |
| Group A (log scale) | partly | log1p of income_btc and neighbors; count and length are missing |
| Group B (ratios) | partly | income / neighbors only; count / neighbors needs count |
| Group C (dollar value) | yes | income_btc times the day's close |
| Group D (round amounts) | yes | computed from income_btc and income_usd |
| count, weight, length, looped | no | need the full day's transaction graph |
| Group E (same-day percentiles) | no | needs every other address active that day |

After removing the 6,617 addresses already in BitcoinHeist, the outside test has 554 addresses from families BitcoinHeist knows and 4,007 from 110 families it does not (counts before the 0.3 BTC filter). The new-family slice is dominated by NotNevada and the `Unlabeled` group; I would report NotNevada as its own row and drop `Unlabeled` from the family slices.

## 2. Feature definitions

Checked against the paper and written up in `data/README.md`. The ones that matter for this project: `income` is the total satoshi paid into the address that day, `neighbors` is the number of transactions that pay into it that day, and `count`, `weight`, `length` and `looped` describe the chains of earlier transactions that lead to the address within the same day, so they need the whole day's transaction graph.

## 7. Price coverage

The Coin Metrics series runs from 2010-07-18 to 2026-11-08 (5,958 days, 0 missing days inside that range, 0 null prices).

BitcoinHeist dates without a price: 0 of 2,885. Ransomwhere bitcoin payment dates (UTC-6 days) without a price: 0 of 2,277.

Coin Metrics `PriceUSD` is a UTC end-of-day reference rate, while BitcoinHeist days are cut in UTC-6. A payment late on a UTC-6 day is priced with the close 6 hours before it happened, which is close enough for dollar amounts rounded to $50 or more.
