## Outside test on Ransomwhere

The portable model uses only what the Ransomwhere payment list can rebuild: `income`, `neighbors`, `log_income_btc`, `log_neighbors`, `avg_payment_btc`, `income_usd`, `log_income_usd`, `round_usd_50`, `round_usd_100`, `round_usd_500`, `round_btc_0.1`, `round_btc_0.5`, `round_btc_1`, `round_min`. It is trained on BitcoinHeist with the final model split and settings, and its threshold is fixed at 0.1% FPR on validation. On the BitcoinHeist test years it reaches PR-AUC 0.019, recall 0.001 and an address-day FPR of 0.06%. 0.06% of white test addresses are flagged on at least one day.

The test set is every Ransomwhere bitcoin address that is not in BitcoinHeist, with payments from 2016-01-12 to 2024-08-19. Repeated payment rows and payments under 0.3 BTC are dropped first, as BitcoinHeist did. An address counts as caught when any of its days crosses the threshold. Overall address recall: 0.031 on 1,084 addresses.

Caveats: `income` and `neighbors` are rebuilt from the Ransomwhere payment list and follow the BitcoinHeist definitions only approximately. BitcoinHeist counts every transaction paying the address that day; Ransomwhere lists only the payments its contributors attributed. Ransomwhere has no benign addresses, so the false-positive rate comes from the BitcoinHeist test years. Addresses with more payment days get more chances to be caught.

Could not be rebuilt: `count`, `weight`, `length`, `looped` (they need the whole day of transactions), `count_per_neighbor`, and Group E (it needs every other address active that day).
